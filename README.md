# CS255-DriverPass-Portfolio

1. Project Summary

The DriverPass project involved designing a system for a driving school that wanted to help students prepare for their DMV driving tests. The client wanted a web-based system that combined driving lesson scheduling with online classes and practice tests. The system needed to let customers manage accounts and appointments, assign drivers and vehicles, track practice-test results, and give employees tools to manage reservations, training packages, and reports.

2. What I Did Well

I think I did particularly well at identifying the client's requirements and organizing them into a system design. I created UML diagrams to show how users interact with the system and how different parts of the system work together. The activity diagrams helped me break down processes such as scheduling driving lessons and taking online practice tests into individual steps. I also developed a sequence diagram and class diagram to show how information moves through the system and how its data can be organized.

3. What I Would Revise

If I could revise one part of my work, I would spend more time reviewing and refining my UML diagrams. I would make sure the labels, relationships, and decision paths were consistent and easy to understand. I would also review the diagrams alongside the business requirements document to confirm that every important client requirement was represented clearly. This would make the design easier for developers to understand and implement.

4. Interpreting and Implementing User Needs

I interpreted the client's needs by identifying the problems DriverPass wanted to solve and translating them into functional and nonfunctional requirements. For example, the need to make lesson scheduling easier became a requirement for customers and the secretary to create, cancel, and modify reservations. The need to track student progress became a requirement to record practice-test scores, time taken, and completion status. I used these requirements to guide my UML diagrams and overall system design.

Considering user needs is important because a system should solve the problems it was created to address. If designers misunderstand the client's expectations, the final product might not provide the necessary features or could be difficult for users to navigate. Understanding user needs from the beginning helps reduce errors, avoid unnecessary work, and create a system that is useful and reliable.

5. My Approach to Software Design

My approach to software design is to understand the problem first, identify the users and their needs, and then determine the requirements before planning the system. In future projects, I would use techniques such as reviewing client interviews, separating functional and nonfunctional requirements, creating UML use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams, and checking the design against the original requirements. I would also seek feedback from others and revise my documentation when needed. These strategies would help me create designs that are organized, understandable, and easier to implement and maintain.
